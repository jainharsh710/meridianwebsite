GIFT City or an overseas account?

India’s offshore centre is growing fast. Here is where it fits, and where it doesn’t yet.

By Harsh Jain & Harsh Bothra · October 2026 · 5 min read

IFSCARegulator for GIFT City, not SEBI
LRSThe route for both options
~50US stocks on NSE IFSC as depository receipts

GIFT City in Gandhinagar is India’s International Financial Services Centre. It is legally treated as offshore: products there are priced in dollars, and resident Indians invest in them through the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, the same route as investing abroad.

What you can buy there today

  • US stock receipts on NSE IFSC. Unsponsored depository receipts that mirror around 50 large US companies, traded in dollars.
  • GIFT City funds. Fund-of-funds from Indian fund houses tracking indices like the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100, outside the $7 billion mutual fund cap.
  • AIFs and portfolio products, often with minimums of $150,000 or more.
  • Dollar deposits with bank branches in GIFT City.

How it compares

GIFT CityOverseas account
RegulatorIFSCA (Indian)The broker’s home regulator, such as the SEC
FundingLRSLRS
RangeLimited and growingGlobal shares, ETFs and bonds across 100+ markets
CostsFund-of-funds carry two layers of feesLow-cost global ETFs, single layer
LiquidityThin in many receiptsDeep, on the world’s largest exchanges
Tax reportingTreated as foreign assets, reported in Schedule FAThe same

Our view

GIFT City is a welcome addition, and some of its products, such as dollar deposits and certain funds, can be useful. But today it does not offer the breadth, liquidity or low-cost global index funds available through an established overseas broker. Importantly, using it does not remove the paperwork: it is still LRS money and still foreign assets in your return.

We watch it closely. As the range deepens, the right mix may include both.
