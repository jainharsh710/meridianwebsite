International investing from India. Any country, one account.

How resident Indians invest in the US, Europe, Japan and the world’s other major markets: the legal route, the four ways to own a country, and what it costs.

India is about 3% of the world’s stock market. The other 97% sits in the United States, China, Japan, Europe and dozens of other countries. International investing means owning part of that 97%, so your wealth doesn’t depend on one economy, one currency or one decade. Here is the full case for investing globally.

Whether you want to invest in the US, invest in Japan, invest in Germany or invest in a single emerging market, the route from India is the same. Only the fund or the exchange changes.

How to invest in any country from India

Every overseas investment by a resident Indian follows the same three steps.

  1. Open an overseas brokerage account in your name. Most direct investors use a global broker such as Interactive Brokers, which gives access to exchanges across North America, Europe and Asia from one account. How to open an Interactive Brokers account from India.
  2. Send money under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme. Your Indian bank wires rupees, converted to dollars, under LRS. TCS of 20% applies above ₹10 lakh a year, and you adjust it when you file your return. LRS, TCS and Schedule FA, explained.
  3. Buy the market you want. Choose a fund or shares that give you exposure to that country, then report the holding in Schedule FA each year.

Four ways to own a country

  • A global fund. One all-world index fund holds thousands of companies across developed and emerging markets, weighted by size. It is the simplest way to invest internationally from India.
  • A regional fund. Europe, Asia-Pacific, or emerging markets in a single holding, for investors who want to tilt towards a region.
  • A single-country fund. Index funds exist for most large markets, from Japan and the UK to Taiwan, Korea and Brazil. They are the cleanest way to invest in one specific country.
  • Individual shares. Buying companies directly on a foreign exchange. It brings the most control and the most concentration risk.

Where the fund is registered matters as much as what it holds. US-domiciled funds can expose your family to US estate tax above $60,000, while Ireland-domiciled UCITS funds generally don’t. US ETFs vs Irish UCITS ETFs.

International investing: questions.

Can I invest in any country from India?
Almost any. Under the RBI’s Liberalised Remittance Scheme, a resident Indian can send up to $250,000 a year abroad for investment and buy shares and funds listed in most of the world’s markets. LRS does not allow remittances to countries the FATF identifies as non-cooperative, and some markets restrict foreign investors.
What is the easiest way to invest in a foreign country’s stock market from India?
For most people, a diversified fund listed on a major exchange, such as a global, regional or single-country index fund, held in a brokerage account in your own name. It gives you the whole market in one holding, without opening an account in that country.
Is international investment legal for resident Indians?
Yes. Overseas investment by resident individuals is permitted under LRS. You declare the purpose to your bank, the bank collects TCS where it applies, and you report foreign holdings in Schedule FA of your income tax return.
How much can I invest abroad from India?
Up to $250,000 per person per financial year under LRS, across all purposes combined. Each family member has their own limit.
How are foreign investments taxed in India?
As a resident, you pay Indian tax on worldwide income. Long-term gains on foreign shares and funds held for more than 24 months are taxed at 12.5% without indexation; shorter-term gains at your slab rate. Dividends are taxed in India, with credit for tax withheld abroad under the relevant treaty.
